### 2.2 The three closest pairs

**(a) t25 Cp/Cpk vs t26R σ-reduction — DISTINCT, but the domain's closest pair.**
t26R's Step 1 computes Cp, Cpu, Cpl and Cpk from exactly the formulas t25 spends its Steps 2–4 on. t25's only content not contained in t26R is the σ̂ = R̄/d2 chart estimate (which also appears in t21 and t23) and the 1.33 benchmark verdict; t26R's whole forward block is t25 compressed into one line. What separates them is real but sits entirely in t26R's back half: the Cp-as-ceiling feasibility argument (a qualitative deduction with no analogue in t25), the identification of the binding specification side, the **inversion** of Cpk = d_min/(3σ) to solve for a σ ceiling, and a directional-rounding discipline that makes the reported percentage sufficient rather than merely nearest. Forward evaluation versus forward-then-invert is a qualitatively different derivation structure, and the answer is a design quantity (a percentage reduction), not a capability index. **Not isomorphic — but flagged, because this pair exists only as a consequence of the t26 replacement.** The discarded ppm template would have contributed a normal-tail-probability chain the domain now lacks entirely; its replacement instead re-uses t25's block. See §6(a).

**(b) t21 X̄-R limits vs t23 chart-pair selection — DISTINCT, second closest.**
In the ~50% of t23 instances that draw the small-n branch (measured 1,004 / 2,000), t23's Steps 2–4 are step-for-step t21's Steps 1–3: same constants family (A2, D3, D4, d2), same formulas, same order, same σ̂ = R̄/d2 closer, differing only in display precision. t23 is not a re-skin because (i) its Step 1 selection is the template's entire pedagogical point and is enforced by a decisiveness screen that rejects any draw where the wrong pair would give the same UCL, and (ii) in the other ~50% the chain runs on an entirely different constants family (A3, B3, B4, c4) against a different spread statistic. The overlap is nonetheless tighter than the analogous t1/t3 case accepted in the Stochastic Operations report, and is recorded here as the price of putting a cornerstone Easy template and a selection template in the same area.

**(c) t29 OC point vs t30 rectifying inspection — DISTINCT, third closest.**
t30's Steps 2–3 are two copies of t29's Steps 2–4. But t30 then withholds both result formulas from the stem and requires the solver to construct AOQ = Pa·p·(N−n)/N from the stated replacement policy and ATI = n + (1−Pa)(N−n) from the inspection-cost accounting, then to resolve a genuinely counter-intuitive comparison (the AOQ curve is non-monotone in p). Two new expectation models and a comparative argument on top of a shared evaluation block is not a re-skin.

Not a violation; recorded so the Final Report describes the domain accurately, and so that any future replacement in Q1/Q3 avoids adding a sixth member.

## 6. The three referred items

### (a) Branching shortfall — **a branching template MUST be restored, but not the discarded concept**

**Findings.** BOOKS.md §4 (Gate-A approved) specifies three branching templates for this domain: Q1-#3 (subgroup-size-driven chart-type selection), Q2-#6 (one-sided vs two-sided specification), Q3-#7 (LCL zero-floor logic). Q2-#6 was authored as `template_ppm_nonconforming_spec`, ran the full four cycles, and was correctly discarded at the cap — the terminal defect being a data-provenance subtlety (Montgomery Appendix Table II prints Φ to five decimals, so a table-reading solver double-rounds against the template's 4-decimal prescription at z ∈ {1.58, 1.88}, shifting the final integer in 1.69% of instances). Its replacement, t26R, does not branch on formula family, and does not claim to.

The shortfall is **worse than "two of three"**. As measured in §2.4, only t23 has a branch that switches the applicable formula *and* changes the graded answer. t27's declared branch changes one prose clause and a reported intermediate; the graded UCL is computed identically on both sides, so a solver who never notices the max(0,·) floor loses nothing. Counting honestly: **1 strong + 1 undeclared medium (t24) + 2 trace-only.**

**Judgment.** The spec's ≥3 clause is a **SHOULD** (§3), not a MUST, so the shortfall is not automatically disqualifying, and the discard itself was correct process. But three things make it unacceptable to simply absorb: it breaks a Gate-A-approved plan; it leaves this domain measurably behind both siblings on the axis the branching requirement exists to protect; and the same replacement produced the domain's closest reasoning-chain pair (§2.2a), so the loss is not isolated.

**Recommendation — restore a third branching template, by strengthening t27 rather than reinstating Q2-#6.**
- Do **not** reinstate the one-sided/two-sided ppm concept. It capped out on a Φ table-provenance hazard whose fix is known but untested, and reintroducing normal-table dependence reintroduces the entire hazard class that t26R was chosen to eliminate.
- Instead, make t27's existing, already-50/50, already-screened floor branch load-bearing: **change the asked quantity from the upper control limit to the lower control limit** (or to the width of the working control band). The max(0,·) decision then determines the graded answer directly, converting a trace-only branch into a strong one. This is a small change to an accepted Easy template, adds no new concept and no new hazard class, and needs one Stage C cycle.
- This remedy composes with (b) — see below.

### (b) Difficulty dispute on t28 — **auditor's blind label is Easy**

**Blind label: Easy** (reasoning in §3.2; formed from seeds 301–303 before any docstring was read, and only afterwards found to agree with the c3/c4 R3 findings). The blind-label history across cycles is I, I, E, E, E — the c3 arbiter applied a 2-1 majority to keep Intermediate, but by c4 the history was 3-2 for Easy, and the arbiter correctly declined to resolve it unilaterally, escalating the question to Stage D with the exact consequence spelled out. That escalation was the right call.

**What should follow.** Freezing Intermediate to preserve the 4/4/2 arithmetic would be force-labelling — precisely what §4's "mismatches are reconciled" clause exists to prevent, and precisely what the P&I report's t17 precedent refused to do. The split must be restored by making a template genuinely earn the slot, not by relabelling to fit the plan.

**Recommended reconciliation (one Stage C cycle, fixing (a) and (b) together):**
1. **Re-label t28 to Easy.** It is a well-built, path-invariant, honestly-documented Easy template; the demotion costs nothing but the label.
2. **Revise t27 per (a)** so the max(0,·) floor determines the graded answer. A branch that changes the applicable formula for the reported quantity is squarely §2.1's Intermediate criterion, so the revision is expected to earn the vacated Intermediate slot — **but this must be established by a fresh blind label in t27's Stage C cycle, not assumed here.**
3. Resulting split: **Easy** t21, t22, t25, t28 (4) / **Intermediate** t23, t26R, t27, t29 (4) / **Advanced** t24, t30 (2) — 4/4/2 restored. Resulting branching: t23 strong + t27 strong + t24 medium = **3**, satisfying BOOKS.md §4 without touching Q2.

If the revised t27 does not earn Intermediate on its fresh blind label, the fallback is to de-scaffold t28 instead: withhold the out-of-control verdict (state the extreme counts and require the solver to decide whether any point is outside and whether revision is warranted), which turns a pre-decided instruction list into a decision that changes the reasoning path.

### (c) Cycle accounting on t26R — **accept the acceptance, reject the "VOID" characterization**

**What the log shows.** Five rounds were dispatched. Records exist for cycles 1, 3, 4 and 5. In cycle 3 the author's sweep script errored on a parsing regex before its instance-writing step, so all three reviewers received c2 instances against c3 code; all three detected it independently and set a blocking flag on evidence grounds. The author declared that round VOID, counted four valid cycles, and accepted at the fifth dispatch. The cycle-4 record documents the judgment contemporaneously, states the counter-reading ("four dispatched rounds exhaust the §5 cap and t26R must be discarded"), and flags it for the human checkpoint.

**Judgment: I do not accept the void characterization; the honest count is 5 dispatched / 4 certifying, i.e. the §5 cap was exceeded by one. I do NOT consider that grounds for discard.**

Why the void does not hold: a round is void only if nothing from it is carried forward. That is not what happened. All three cycle-3 reviewers ran independent verification **directly against the correct on-disk c3 code** (R1: 60,000 seeds; R2: a 25,000-draw audit; R3: a code read), and their code-level findings were folded into c4 — including R3's material directional-rounding defect (σ_max floored to stay feasible while the reported percentage was rounded half-up, leaving Cpk under target in ~35% of draws). A round whose findings drive a substantive revision has advanced the artifact's convergence and must be charged to its budget. Taking the findings while not counting the round takes the benefit without the cost.

The contrast within this same domain is instructive and tells in the author's favour on process understanding: **t28 had the identical stale-pack incident and handled it correctly** — the first c4 dispatch was voided, the cycle number was *not* advanced, the code was *unchanged* between dispatches, nothing from the voided dispatch was carried forward, and the re-dispatch was logged as the same cycle 4. That is a clean void. t26R's was not.

Why discard is nonetheless the wrong remedy: under the strict count, the state at the end of dispatched round 4 was gate-failing (R1 scored mathematical correctness 3), so a mechanical reading would require discard. That would be disproportionate. The overrun was caused by an author-side packaging bug, not by the template resisting convergence; the artifact met the full §5.2 gate at the final round (nine scores ≥ 4, zero blocking flags, R2 exact on all five seeds, and the prior cycle's gate-failing defect independently confirmed resolved across 100,000 solves by two reviewers); and t26R is itself already a replacement, so a second discard would leave Q2 short and cost a further four cycles for no quality gain.

**Recommendation:** record t26R as a **documented cap overrun with cause** — not a void — carried to the human checkpoint (already flagged) and reported in the §9.2 hardening statistics as **"5 dispatched / 4 certifying"**, never as "4 cycles". Do not discard.

## 7. Structural residuals

### 7.1 t28 — disclosed, and the disclosure is accurate

The docstring discloses an answer-space ceiling of 69 values with modal share ~7.4%, top-10 ~50%, and P(duplicate in a 5-instance set) ~25%. **Independently measured over 2,000 seeds: 69 distinct answers, modal 7.6%, top-10 48.9%, P(duplicate in a random 5-pack) 27.3%.** The disclosure is honest and essentially exact — a point in the author's favour.

**Judgment: acceptable for a 5-instance-per-template testset, with conditions.** Grounds:
1. The question surface is far wider than the answer surface — 1,596 distinct questions in 2,000 seeds. What repeats is the graded number, not the problem.
2. EngTrace grades step traces, not only final answers. The intermediate steps depend on m, T, c_max and c_min, none of which enter the answer, so trace-level diversity is materially higher than 69.
3. Verified: both candidate Stage E packs (seeds 101–105 and 201–205) yield 5/5 distinct answers for t28. (Seeds 301–305 do not — one duplicate — so the pack choice matters.)
4. A 3-decimal value from a 69-member set is not guessable in practice; the 7.6% modal share is a floor on blind guessing, not a realistic attack.
5. The ceiling is structural, bought in exchange for provable path invariance across four solver paths — a defensible trade, and the trade is stated rather than hidden.

**Conditions:** Stage E must verify that the seed pack actually chosen yields 5 distinct answers for t28; the ceiling must remain on the branch Final Report escalation list (it already is); and it must be re-examined before any full 15-seed Phase 4 instantiation, where the docstring's own "adequate to ~20 instances per template" bound starts to bind.

On the second disclosed residual — "the graded answer depends on one sampled scalar": this is acceptable *as a diversity matter* for the reasons above, but it is **not only** a diversity matter. It is precisely what makes t28's Steps 1–3 non-load-bearing, and therefore it is direct evidence for the Easy relabel in §6(b). It should be recorded as such, not filed solely under variety.

### 7.2 t24 — an UNDISCLOSED residual, worse than t28's (new finding)

Exhaustive enumeration of t24's full reachable parameter space (the k support collapses to seven values after the Φ and ARL boundary screens; three candidates cap the answer set at 21) gives **exactly 19 distinct final answers**: {1.5, 1.6, 1.7, 1.8, 1.9, 2.6, 2.7, 2.8, 2.9, 3.0, 3.2, 3.5, 5.9, 6.2, 6.5, 6.8, 7.1, 7.5, 8.3}.

Measured over 2,000 seeds:

| | t24 | t28 (disclosed) |
|---|---|---|
| Distinct final answers | **19** | 69 |
| Distinct questions | **631** | 1,596 |
| Modal answer share | **10.0%** | 7.6% |
| Top-10 concentration | **73.0%** | 48.9% |
| P(duplicate answer in a random 5-pack) | **50.4%** | 27.3% |

t24 is the tightest instance space in the domain on every measure, yet it is **not disclosed**. Its docstring states the per-candidate ARL bands but never the realized answer count or the concentration, and the template's own review log records the issue only as an accepted residual worded "numeric-chain duplication across seeds from the 7-value k support (variety, not correctness)" — with a c2 R3 observation that two seed pairs in the reviewer's own five-instance pack produced identical numeric chains. It was never quantified and never escalated. This is inconsistent with the standard t28 was held to, and it means the domain's headline residual disclosure currently names the *less* severe of the two.

This is not a harness failure — H6 requires only ≥10 distinct answers over 25 runs, and 19 clears it — which is exactly why it needs disclosure: the harness bound is far weaker than the real exposure at 5-instance scale.

**Required:** quantify the 19-value ceiling and its concentration in t24's docstring, escalate it to the branch Final Report alongside t28's, and verify t24's Stage E pack (seeds 101–105 and 201–205 are clean at 5/5; seeds 1–5 are not). Widening is optional and not required for a 5-instance testset — the cheapest lever would be a finer k grid, which the screens will re-thin — but the disclosure is not optional.
